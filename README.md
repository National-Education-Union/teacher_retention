# Retention of teachers by school type

The National Education Union (NEU) has found that local authority maintained schools retain teachers better than academies and free schools. This pattern is true irrespective of school phase. The NEU also found that local authority maintained schools and single academy trusts retain teachers better than multi-academy trusts (MATs) and that large MATs have the worst record of teacher retention.

## Methodology
The NEU acquired data for teacher retention at school level through a freedom of information request to the Department for Education . The data release gave the FTE of teachers, the FTE of those moving school, and the FTE of those leaving the profession for each school for each academic year from the 2010-11 school year until the 2022-23 school year. This data was taken from the School Workforce Census. This data was then combined with other school level data, including location, governance, and phase, in order to make comparisons between groups of similar schools. At points this analysis refers to Small, Medium, and Large MATs. We classified MATs containing 2-5 schools as small, those containing 6-20 schools as medium, and those containing at least 21 schools as large. 

## Findings
MATs generally had lower retention rates and higher rates of teachers leaving the profession than locally maintained schools. Among large MATs, several had retention rates of 75% or lower in the 2022-23 school year, meaning that at least 1 in 4 members of staff left at schools in these MATs. The large MAT with the lowest retention rate (71.9%) was Unity Schools Partnership, followed by RISE Multi Academy Trust (72.4%), Harris Federation (72.5%), ARK Schools (73.0%), Reach2 Academy Trust (74.1%), The Diocese of Chelmsford Vine Schools Trust (74.2%), E-ACT (74.5%), and The White Horse Federation (74.9%). Many of this years worst MATs for teacher retention have been consistently underperforming on retention compared to other MATs, and all had a rate of staff leaving the profession entirely of at least 12% (higher than the average for large MATs) in 2022-23. 
The proportion of teachers who are leaving the profession annually has increased substantially since a low point during the Covid-19 pandemic. Throughout the last 10 years, MATs have consistently had higher rates of teachers leaving the profession than single academies or LA maintained schools. This difference is particularly striking when comparing large MATs, those containing at least 21 schools, to other school types. In the 2022-23 academic year, more than 1 in 10 teachers at large MATS (10.7%) left the profession. Even the best performing school types (local authority maintained schools and single academies) saw left profession rates of around 9%, which reveals the depth of the retention crisis across the sector.

Special schools have the highest rates of teachers leaving the profession and secondary schools have the lowest, but phase seems to be a much less important factor than governance type. Within each phase, the rate at which teachers leave the profession is highest in free schools, followed by Academies, and local authority-maintained schools have the lowest rates of teachers leaving the profession. In free special schools, the rate of leaving the profession is an alarming 13.4%, compared to 9.1% in local authority maintained special schools.

Teachers in schools with higher levels of pupil eligibility for free school meals are more likely to leave the profession. This trend is particularly pronounced among secondary schools. In primary schools with the fewest pupils who were eligible for free school meals, 8.9% of teachers left the profession, compared to 8.6% in secondary schools. Among primary schools with the most pupils eligible for free school meals, 10.0% of teachers left the profession, compared to 10.6% in secondary schools.
